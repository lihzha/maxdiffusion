exp_03 rollout_objective — Results Report

Frozen-eval question: do losses that train on the 25-step rollout trajectory (A: corrective scheduled sampling · B: short-horizon rollout · C: λ·A+(1−λ)·B) beat one-step denoising on OVERFIT100?  ·  One training seed, canonical-100 windows, seed-0 rollouts  ·  Closed 2026-08-08.

Yixun's closing verdict (Query 8): from scratch, C (λ=0.5) works better than the A and B methods. The predeclared +0.02 practical-effect hard gate was NOT met by any arm (best ≈ 40% of it) — by his adjudication the experiment closes rather than continues. Code unmerged per SOP.

1 · Instruments (why these numbers are citable)

  • Certified control: ctrl0 (the exp_03 trainer running the exp_02 recipe by identity) reproduced exp_02's training to ~1e-11 on losses at 3 anchored checkpoints and 2.5e-11 on SSIM — trainer drift is excluded by measurement.
  • The loss→SSIM law (exact OLS on exp_02's one-step checkpoints: SSIM = 0.98895405 − 1.20004257·loss): 9 held-out evaluations within ±0.005, controls in this experiment at −0.0002 and ≈0 residual. Held-out, not statistically independent (shared ancestry). Every "off-law" number below = measured SSIM − law prediction.
  • Exact seed anchors: every checkpoint handoff reproduced its fixed-RNG instrument value to 7 decimals (e.g. 0.1222672 at 10,000; 0.1186258 at A's 12,500).

The trial objectives are most profitable exactly where exp_02 stalled: at a trained checkpoint whose one-step loss has flattened. From init the rollout signal must be paid for; C's blend halves the bill. At the trained state A's signal is free — blending only dilutes it. Recipe implication (untested): λ scheduled from 0.5 toward 1 as training matures.

Honest limits: one training seed and one rollout seed everywhere (t-statistics measure per-episode consistency within that seed); the +0.02 hard gate unmet by every arm; the law applied to trial arms is a reference counterfactual, not a causal price; "cancellation" is a hypothesis; two closely-spaced points cannot establish a trend for A's lead. One arm-mixing arithmetic error in the original growth claim was caught by the Codex analysis review and corrected with disclosure (results doc, RESULT 9).
